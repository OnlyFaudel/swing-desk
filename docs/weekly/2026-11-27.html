The week in nuclear

The sector is weak despite supportive commodity prices. The basket is about 2.4% below its 10-day average, so the desk is blocked from new buys. LEU is down 24% over a month and 52% over a quarter; CEG is 15.5% below its 20-day high.

Asia's close pointed to gains of roughly 1% at the open. We treat that as information only, since it predicts the opening gap and nothing after.

  • Thanksgiving week bounce helped, but the basket has not reclaimed its trend line.
  • Black Friday to Monday has historically been weak for the power names (down 6 of 7 years since 2019).

Uranium and the fuel chain

Spot is $91.55 and long-term contracts are $98.00, an all-time high for that series. Spot sits $6.45 below long-term, so buyers are still contracting at higher prices than the day-to-day market. Spot is well under the $136 all-time high and the $100.25 five-year high.

The Sprott trust holds about 81.7 million pounds and trades at $17.73 against a NAV of $21.53. At a discount it cannot raise money to buy uranium, so it is no help to spot prices. A weekly change in the discount was not available.

  • Spot rose from $75.80 in Nov 2025 to $91.55 now.
  • Headline noise this week: a Kashiwazaki restart delay in Japan, a new Taishan unit in China, and Energy Fuels' White Mesa production target.

Plants and power

The US fleet is 90.2% running. Among our owners only Salem 1 (CEG) is down, in a planned outage that began Oct 4 and is now 52 days old. It is refueling season, so this is normally routine, but we have not confirmed it is on schedule. Vistra and Talen units are all running.

  • Recent outages at Byron 2, Peach Bottom 2, Comanche Peak 1, Dresden 3 and Susquehanna 1 have all ended.
  • CEG: Constellation-Google deal commentary resurfaced; this is old news and already priced in.

Policy and deals

Regulators advanced two plants meant to reduce reliance on Russian fuel, including a Federal Register notice for General Matter's Paducah enrichment facility. That could eventually compete with Centrus (LEU), but the impact is unconfirmed and far off. The Russian enriched uranium ban allows exceptions only until Jan 1, 2028.

Our files show LEU's past deal headlines were followed by a median -4.65% over 5 days, so we do not chase them. Analysts rate LEU a buy on average with a mean target of $180, but JP Morgan sits at Neutral with a $100 target.

  • No relevant congressional action this week; the bills in the feed are 2008-2009 items.

What the desk did

Ten Thanksgiving run-up trades (about $15 each) closed after two sessions: nine won, led by UUUU +10.0%, DNN +9.2% and CCJ +6.9%. CEG lost 3.1%. Larger panic-rebound buys also paid: UUUU +$16.09, VST +$8.97, LEU +$5.83. A CEG patient-level trade was closed at a $0.11 loss because no stop could be placed.

The honest part: the Thanksgiving and panic-rebound buys skipped our rule against buying while the basket is below its 10-day average. They made money, but we got lucky. Calendar and rebound setups now have to pass that check first.

  • Account $1,040.90 vs $1,000 at the start of the week.
  • Two scout calls were wrong, UEC and CCJ, likely bad luck, though one was based on a stale recap.

What we're watching next

Whether the basket climbs back above its 10-day average, which unlocks new buys. Also whether Salem 1 returns on schedule, and any follow-up on the Paducah facility and its read-through to LEU.

  • CEG: does the $236.82 zone hold?
